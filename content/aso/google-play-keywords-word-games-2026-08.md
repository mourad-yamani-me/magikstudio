Eighteen open terms out of ten thousand keywords; eight under 30 on
[Rankgrip](/aso/rankgrip/). The thinnest report in the August set by a wide
margin — and the one where the method visibly struggles.

It goes out anyway, deliberately. A series that only shows the categories where
its measurements look good is not measuring anything.

## What it gets wrong

The two highest-demand open terms: `arrow puzzle master cash` at Rankgrip 11,
`arrow puzzle masters` at 24. Neither is a word game. They appear because the
apps holding their top ten are listed under Word alongside three or four other
categories — the attribution follows the listing, not the game.

Sixteen of the eighteen rows also appear in the casual report. Two are exclusive
to word games. When eighty-nine percent of a report is borrowed, the category
label is doing very little work.

The cause is the mechanism that serves every other category well: a keyword takes
the categories of the apps ranking for it, because Play keywords carry no
category of their own and there is no other signal. In a large category the
borrowed rows are diluted by genuine ones. In a category this size they are most
of the report.

## What it gets right

Three rows survive that reading. `reel guess movie app` at Rankgrip 29, with
demand of 71 and ReelGuess at number one, is a real guessing-game term.
`imposter challenge guess who` at 16 has the second-lowest grip in the report and
a fifteen-month-old leader. `chameleon party` at 41 is contested but genuine.

The pattern across those three is guessing, not spelling. Play answers
word-adjacent searches with social deduction and trivia — crosswords barely
feature. `guess who 2 player offline` at 31 belongs to the same family, and
appears in the board report too.

If you build word games, that is the useful signal in an otherwise weak issue:
**the demand near your category is for guessing games played with other people**,
and the vocabulary that reaches it is "guess", "who", "party", "imposter" rather
than "word" or "puzzle".

None of the three has a big developer holding more than a couple of the ten
slots, and none has an exact title match. On the numbers they are as open as
anything in the month's stronger reports. There are simply three of them.

## The wall

`word puzzle` sits at Rankgrip 82 — the highest score in any August report. A
hundred million installs, and the phrase sitting in the app's own title. That is
what a closed term looks like: the clearest case this month of a category's own
name being unavailable to newcomers.

`puzzle lovers` follows at 53 and `teasers` at 62. `each` at 72 is a stray — a
common word that ranks a voice chat app, and the sort of row that survives every
filter and still means nothing.

## What comes next for this category

Word games needs a longer measurement window before its report is worth much.
The next issue will have two full months to compare and a better chance of
separating genuine word-game terms from borrowed ones. Until then, read this one
as eighteen rows with three that matter.
