Fifty-two thousand keywords put a puzzle game into the US Play top ten between
9 July and 31 August. Sixty-one of them survive a gate that asks only two
things: does anybody actually search this, and is anybody big already standing
on it. That is roughly one term in eight hundred, and the ratio is the part of
app store optimisation that keyword tools have no reason to show you — the
number that sells a subscription is the size of the list, not how much of it is
reachable.

Every row below carries a [Rankgrip](/aso/rankgrip/) score: how tightly the term
is already held, 0 to 100, over big-developer slot share, exact title matches,
leader maturity and leader tenure. The sixty-one open terms score between 5 and
41. The eight wall terms score between 47 and 80. Those two sets were chosen by
different rules and they do not overlap, which is the closest thing to a check
this method has.

## The arrow games opened a door, and it is closing

Eight of the sixty-one terms are arrow-puzzle phrases, and not one of them is
held by an app older than fourteen months. `game lucky arrows` is the clearest
case in the whole report: a Rankgrip of 5, one of its ten results held by a big
developer, and the app at number one shipped four months ago. `arrow go 2026`
looks much the same at eight months.

That pattern has a short life. A genre where every leader is new is a genre that
opened recently and has not yet been noticed by studios with user-acquisition
budgets, and the arrow cluster is now large enough — eight distinct phrases with
real autocomplete behind them — that it will be. If you are going to take one of
these, the useful window is measured in months, not years. By the next report
with a full month of comparison behind it, the tenure column here should already
be moving.

The second thing worth reading off the list is how few terms are genuinely
uncontested. Thirty-two of the sixty-one score under 30 — genuinely open — and the
remaining twenty-nine are a fight. "Winnable" here means a term a better listing
could take, not an empty field. There are almost no empty fields in puzzle games.

## What the shape of the phrases tells you

Twenty-five of the terms are three words and sixteen are two; the rest run
longer. Nothing shorter than two words made it through. Single words on Play
belong to whoever bought them years ago, and the wall below makes that concrete:
`puzzles` scores 90 for demand, a Rankgrip of 75, and ten out of ten of its
results belong to developers a small studio is not going to outrank. `satisfying`
is worse at 80, because six apps also carry the word in their title. `place
blocks` is the interesting one: a Rankgrip of only 47 despite Block Blast! and
its billion installs sitting on it, because not one app has put the phrase in a
title. That is a wall with a gap in it.

That is why the wall is printed at all. A list that showed only the open terms
would read like every other keyword post, and it would be quietly dishonest
about what the top of this category looks like.

## What this sample cannot tell you yet

Rankings have been measured daily since 9 July, which is long enough to say who
holds a term and not long enough to say whether their grip is loosening. Month-over-month
movement is the column this report wants most and the one it has not earned; it
arrives when there are two full months to compare rather than one and a half.

One caution about reading the open terms. A low number in the *top 10*
column means only that nobody big is there. Some of these phrases are people
hunting one specific app that happens not to dominate its own results, and a
listing rewrite cannot borrow that intent. Read the whole row — Rankgrip, demand and
tenure together — before spending a week on any of them. [What Rankgrip
measures](/aso/rankgrip/) sets out the formula in full.
