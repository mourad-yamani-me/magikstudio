Board games returns twenty-eight open terms out of twenty-two thousand keywords,
twelve of them under 30 on [Rankgrip](/aso/rankgrip/). That is thin by the
standards of this month's set. The thinness is the finding.

## Almost nothing here is a board game

The highest-demand open term is `fun arrow` at 75, with a Rankgrip of 15. Then
`arrow through` at 29, `guess who 2 player offline` at 31, `hidden colors` at 29,
`arrow go free` at 15. Four of the top five are arrow-routing puzzles.

They land here because the apps holding their top ten are listed under Board —
which on Play has become the shelf for anything played in turns, or against
another person. The recurring holders say the same thing: `Dé Double` and
`डाइस रोलर!` hold three terms each, `Memoria` two. A dice roller and a memory
game are the closest this category gets to a real board-game presence.

**Play does not have a board game search market.** It has a dice-and-party-game
market wearing the label. Ship an actual board adaptation and you will not be
competing with other board adaptations. You will be competing with two-player
mini-game collections, and your listing should be written for that fight.

## Where the room is

`arrow go 2026` has the lowest grip in the report at 10, with an eight-month-old
leader. `fun arrow` and `arrow go free` both sit at 15 against fourteen-month-old
apps. `block fill puzzle offline` is at 16 with demand of 64 and a leader under
three years old.

One term behaves the way the category name suggests: `guess who 2 player
offline`, Rankgrip 31, demand 72, with `2 Player Games - Offline` established at
the top. The phrase describes exactly what a board game does. A fight, then, not
an opening. If board games are what you build, that is the honest target.

Ten of the twenty-eight are two words, eleven are three. A short tail compared
with simulation. That reflects a smaller category, not an easier one.

## The wall

`puzzles` at Rankgrip 75 and `game for puzzle` at 71 head it, both held by a
jigsaw app past a hundred million installs. `block shapes` sits at 59 with Block
Blast! on it, and `offline play` at 59.

Nothing on this wall is a board game either. The terms that reach board-game
audiences at scale belong to jigsaw and block puzzle apps — the same story the
open rows tell, from the other end.

One more thing about those repeat holders: `Dé Double` and `डाइस रोलर!` are both
dice rollers, and neither is listed in English — while this report measures the
US store, in English. Utilities with non-English listings are holding US
board-game slots. That happens when the competing supply is thin enough that
Play reaches for whatever matches.

## What this issue shares

Sixteen of these twenty-eight rows also appear in the puzzle report and twelve in
casual. Four are exclusive to board games. That makes it the least independent
report in the August set, and for a category this size the overlap is itself
information: the audience is very nearly the puzzle audience.
