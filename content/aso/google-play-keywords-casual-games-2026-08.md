Casual returns eighty-seven open terms from a category of nearly sixty thousand
keywords — second only to simulation this month — and fifty-two of them score
under 30 on [Rankgrip](/aso/rankgrip/). On the numbers, the richest hunting
ground in the August set.

One figure complicates that: of the eighty-seven rows, **eight appear in no other
report this month**. Thirty-eight are shared with puzzle, thirty-three with
simulation, twenty-three with arcade, sixteen with word games. Nine percent of
this issue is exclusively casual.

## Casual is a shelf, not a genre

No flaw in the measurement. It is the most useful thing this month's data says
about the category.

Keywords take the categories of the apps holding their top ten. A block puzzle
listed as both Puzzle and Casual puts its terms in both reports; a merge game
listed as Casual and Simulation does the same. Casual is the second label almost
every light mobile game reaches for, so its keyword set becomes the union of
everything around it.

The consequence for a studio is direct: **ranking for a casual term usually means
ranking against a puzzle game or a merge game**, and the competitors waiting
there are the ones you would have met in the narrower category anyway. Picking
"casual" as your niche does not pick you a smaller fight.

## The arrow cluster is here too

`fun arrow` leads on demand at 75 with a Rankgrip of 15. `arrow through` follows
at 29. `game lucky arrows` is the lowest grip in the report at 5, with an app
four months old at number one, and `arrow go 2026` sits at 10 with an
eight-month-old leader.

The same cluster appeared in puzzle, in arcade, in board games. A genre of
arrow-routing puzzles opened recently, its leaders are all young, and those
games list themselves across four categories — so the terms surface in four
reports. Take one and you compete in all four at once.

`chicken roll game` at Rankgrip 7 is the other standout, with a leader thirteen
months old and demand of 56.

## Repeat holders

`Aippy: créer un jeu` holds six of these terms, `AIKO: AI Girlfriend 3D Game`
five, `1000 Offline Games in 1` four. Between them, fifteen of the eighty-seven
rows answer to three apps, none of which set out to make a casual game.
Watch that last one. A compilation ranks by
containing a hundred small games, so it surfaces wherever small games are
searched for while competing properly on none of them. A real game beats it on
the specific term.

## The wall

`satisfying` at Rankgrip 80 and `puzzles` at 75 head it, both held by apps past a
hundred million installs. `matching colors` sits at 49 with Color Match on it,
and `game for puzzle` at 71.

`matching colors` scores lower than most of the wall despite eight of its ten
results belonging to big developers, and the reason is simple: nobody has put the
phrase in a title. Same gap `place blocks` showed in the puzzle report. That is
the shape of a wall worth testing before you accept it.
